# Differentiation for Hebrew-speaking 5th graders learning 8 nature words

## 1. Does differentiated instruction (DI) work?
- **Primary school, meta-analysis (21 studies since 1995):** differentiation as part of a broader approach and computerised differentiation tools had small-to-moderate positive effects on achievement; ability grouping alone was not enough. Deunk et al. 2018, *Educational Research Review* 24 — https://doi.org/10.1016/j.edurev.2018.02.002 (summary read via https://core.ac.uk/download/518737032.pdf)
- **Secondary, systematic review:** mostly small-to-moderate positive effects (d ≈ +0.51 to +0.74), with big knowledge gaps. Smale-Jacobse et al. 2019, *Frontiers in Psychology* — https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6883934
- **EFL specifically:** small recent meta-analyses report very large effects of DI on English achievement (d ≈ 1.6–1.9), larger in primary than secondary, but from 8–18 small studies; treat the size with caution, the direction is consistent. https://dergipark.org.tr/en/download/article-file/4541645 ; https://garuda.kemdiktisaintek.go.id/documents/detail/6524085
- **Take-away:** DI helps when the *task* changes with the child's current level, not just the seat. Done on laptops, it is the "computerised tool" kind Deunk found effective.

## 2. Learning styles: checked, and not the basis for our levels
His idea ("some kids prefer art, some are more logical, some prefer discussion") is the learning-styles *matching* (meshing) hypothesis. The evidence:
- **Pashler, McDaniel, Rohrer & Bjork 2008:** a proper test needs a crossover interaction; they found virtually no studies showing one and "no adequate evidence base to justify incorporating learning-styles assessments into general educational practice". https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/26162104/
- **Rogowsky, Calhoun & Tallal 2015 / 2020:** auditory vs visual learners taught in their preferred mode did no better. https://www.frontiersin.org/articles/10.3389/fpsyg.2020.00164/pdf
- **Clinton-Lisell & Litzinger 2024 meta-analysis** (21 studies, 1,712 participants): a small pooled effect (g = 0.31) from low-quality studies, and only 26% of outcomes showed the required crossover; "too small and too infrequent to warrant widespread adoption". https://www.frontiersin.org/articles/10.3389/fpsyg.2024.1428732/full
- **About 89% of teachers still believe it** (Newton & Salvi 2020 systematic review) — https://www.frontiersin.org/article/10.3389/feduc.2020.602451
- **ELT-specific critique:** https://my.chartered.college/?p=6175

**What we do instead:** sort the three levels by **readiness** (how much English the kid can read and produce today), and give **every** kid a **choice of activity type** (draw / puzzle / talk) inside his level. Preferences become a *choice*, not a diagnosis; everyone gets pictures + sound + text (multimodal, which helps all learners, as Clinton-Lisell & Litzinger note it is a separate idea from matching).

## 3. Student choice inside a level
- **Patall, Cooper & Robinson 2008 meta-analysis** (41 studies): choice raises intrinsic motivation, effort, task performance and perceived competence; effects are stronger for **children**, and benefits fade with too many options — best around **2–4 options**. https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/18298272/ (full text: https://selfdeterminationtheory.org/wp-content/uploads/2019/10/2008_PatallCooperRobinson_PsychBulletin.pdf)
- **For us:** 3 choices per level (🎨 draw it, 🧩 solve it, 🗣️ say it), so 3 levels × 3 doors, but a kid only ever sees his own 3.

## 4. Pictures, sound, games for vocabulary
- Pairing word + picture improves vocabulary retention in young learners (dual coding), but only when the picture maps clearly onto the word. https://doi.org/10.22049/jalda.2018.26275.1076 ; caveat: https://daviddidau.substack.com/p/the-dual-coding-delusion
- Digital game-based vocabulary learning shows positive effects, often seen as most motivating for younger and lower-proficiency learners. https://hrmars.com/papers_submitted/13266/the-effectiveness-of-game-based-learning-strategies-on-primary-esl-learners-vocabulary-learning.pdf ; https://www.atlantis-press.com/article/126024760.pdf

## 5. Where these kids are: Israeli curriculum
- 5th grade sits in the Ministry's elementary curriculum at Pre-A1 → A1 (Pre-basic / Basic user I), with Can-Do statements by domain (interaction, reception, production). https://meyda.education.gov.il/files/Mazkirut_Pedagogit/English/curriculum2020Elementary.pdf ; https://pop.education.gov.il/tchumey_daat/english/yesodi/curriculum/introduction/
- The 5th-grade national test (Meitzav) is built on those Can-Dos. https://meyda.education.gov.il/files/Rama/meitzav20/Eng_LangH_5_2020_Mifrat.pdf

## 6. Three readiness levels and their scaffolds (our proposal for the design)
| | Level 1 — "still decoding" | Level 2 — "reads words" | Level 3 — "reads sentences" |
|---|---|---|---|
| Who | Pre-A1; reads letters slowly, needs Hebrew | A1; reads single words, short phrases | A1+; reads and writes short sentences |
| Input | picture + clip on every word, Hebrew help line, tap-to-hear | picture + word, clip on demand | words + short sentences, clip on demand |
| Task | match picture↔sound, build the word from letter tiles | complete short phrases ("a dry ___"), sort words | write/say a sentence or tiny riddle with the words |
| 🎨 / 🧩 / 🗣️ choice | colour the scene by word / memory pairs / say-and-record with a friend | label a drawn map / word puzzle / describe to a partner | design a postcard / riddle chain / mini-interview |
| Support fades | always on | one tap away | only on request |

Evidence base for this shape: readiness-tiered tasks (§1), choice of 3 (§3), multimodal input for all (§2, §4), L1 support kept for the weakest group only where they need it to act (the lesson skill's rule).

Limit, said plainly: no study here tested exactly this design with Hebrew-speaking 5th graders; it is assembled from the general findings above.
