## 1. The lesson plan

### Header

- **Main objective:** By the end of the lesson, pupils can say, read and spell eight nature words (air, dry, forest, grass, mountain, nature, river, sea) and use them in one short spoken sentence.
- **Global Can-Do (Pre-A1 to A1):** can recognise familiar words with pictures; can understand short, simple instructions given slowly with gestures; can produce short phrases on a familiar topic.
- The global wording is from memory of the CEFR descriptors; match it to the Ministry curriculum PDF linked in differentiation.md §5 before sending. [inferred]
- **Operative Can-Do, "I can…":**
  1. say the eight words when I see the picture or the gesture;
  2. find the right picture or word when I hear or read it;
  3. build each word from letters;
  4. say "Let's go to the sea!" about a place I choose.
- **On screen for kids:** "I can say, read and spell eight nature words." (already `LESSON.goal`, index.html:150).
- **Materials:** the site, the teacher view (`#teacher`), the Classroom post, this plan.

### Stage 1: Welcome to nature (4 min, laptops closed)

- **Projector:** the existing intro screen with title, goal and agenda (index.html:455-457).
- **Say:**
  - "Good morning! Laptops closed. Eyes on me."
  - "Look. Trees, water, a big sky. This is nature." (open arms)
  - "Who likes a trip to the sea? Hands up! Who likes a trip to the mountains?"
  - "You know nature words in English. Tell me! What is in nature?" Write six to eight on the board (tree, sun, flower, water, bird); the class repeats each once.
  - "Today: eight NEW nature words. Read with me." The class reads the goal aloud.
  - "First we meet the words. Then you find your trail on the laptop. Then we talk. Then a game."
  - "Who can say in Hebrew what we do today?" One pupil says it.
- **Every kid:** raises a hand, calls out words, repeats, reads the goal.
- **If silent:** draw a sun and a tree on the board: "What is this?"
- **Transition:** "Ready to meet the words? Stand up!"
- **Praise:** "You know so many nature words already. Great start!"

### Stage 2: Meet the words (9 min, standing)

- **Projector:** the existing word cards; Space shows and says the word, → moves on (index.html:459-466). Add a ninth slide with all eight pictures and no words.
- **Order:** nature, mountain, river, forest, sea, grass, air, dry. It keeps look-alike meanings apart (river/sea, forest/grass).
- **Same routine for every word, about 35 seconds:**
  1. "Look. Listen." Press Space: the child clip says the word and the word appears.
  2. "Mountain." (gesture) "Mountain."
  3. "Everybody: mountain!" three times with the gesture: normal, whisper, loud.
  4. "What is 'mountain' in Hebrew?" The class answers together.
  5. "One more time, with your hands!"
- **Gestures** (as in `LESSON.words`, index.html:153-160):
  - air: blow on your hand
  - dry: wipe your forehead
  - forest: arms up like trees
  - grass: wiggle your fingers low
  - mountain: hands meet in a peak
  - nature: open your arms wide
  - river: hand swims like a snake
  - sea: big waves with both arms
- **Two quick games on the ninth slide (3 min):**
  - **Show me!** "I say the word. You show me with your hands. No talking! River! Sea! Dry!" Get faster.
  - **Say it!** "Now I show. You say." Point at a picture or do a gesture silently; the class says the word. End with a trick: point at the mountain and ask "Sea?" The class answers "No! Mountain!"
- **Every kid:** says each word at least four times with the gesture, then gestures, then names.
- **Transition:** "Sit down, please. Now the words go into your laptop. Don't open it yet. Look at the screen."
- **Praise:** "Beautiful gestures, and I heard everyone's voice!"

### Stage 3: Find your trail (5 min)

- **Projector:** the existing three-trails slide (index.html:467-469). Then open the kid link in a second tab and demo one quiz question and one "Listen. Say the word." with the microphone.
- **Introducing the trails, exact words:** "Look. Three trails: River, Forest and Mountain. All three have the same eight words. All three end at the same flag. First, eight quick questions. Then the laptop gives you a trail. It is a good place for YOU to start today. Your trail can change later, and that is OK. One rule: your trail is yours. Eyes on your own screen."
- **Demo:** "Watch me. I listen. I press the picture. No right or wrong here, just try. When you see the microphone, press Allow."
- **Instructions, on four fingers:** "One: open your laptop. Two: go to Classroom, 'Nature Trails'. Three: press the link. Four: headphones on, press Start."
- **Check:** "Alone or with a friend?" (Alone.) "Who can say the four steps in Hebrew?" Then "Go!"
- **Every kid:** opens the link, answers eight questions, sees his trail, starts.
- **Transition:** the reveal leads straight into the trail; switch the projector to stage 4.
- **Praise:** "Quick and quiet. Thank you! I see everyone on a trail."

### Stage 4: On the trails (16 min, alone)

- **Projector:** the eight pictures with their words, and a 16-minute bar moving left to right. The built screen is static text (index.html:471-473).
- **Every kid:** four short games on his trail, then the Last check, then "Mark as done".
- **About 12 minutes in:** "Look, a flag on your screen! Finish your word. Then press the flag: four last questions."
- **At 15 minutes:** "One more minute. After the flag, go to Classroom and press 'Mark as done'."
- **Transition:** "Headphones off. Screens half down, like this. Eyes on me. Now we talk!"
- **Praise (one kid at a time):** "You tried again and you got it. That is how we learn."

### Stage 5: Let's go! (5 min, the one whole-class speaking moment)

- **Projector (new):** two big pictures side by side and the line "Let's go to the ___!" Three slides: sea or mountain; river or forest; all four places.
- **Say, per slide:**
  - "We are going on a class trip! The sea or the mountain? Think. Show me with your hands!"
  - "Now say it, everybody: Let's go to the…!" Each kid says his own choice.
  - "Sea people, stand up! Tell them: Let's go to the sea!" Then the mountain people.
  - To one or two volunteers: "Why the sea?" Accept one word and say it back in full: "Yes, the sea is fun. Everybody: the sea is fun!"
  - Last slide: "Four places, one trip. On three, say your sentence, loud! One, two, three!" Then: "I hear… the sea! Our class trip is to the sea!"
- **Every kid:** gestures, says about six sentences, stands with his group. Trails play no part.
- **Transition:** "Sit down. Last game! Look at all eight pictures."
- **Praise:** "Full sentences from everyone. That is real English!"

### Stage 6: What's missing? (4 min, closing)

- **Projector (new):** the eight pictures without words. Space hides one; Space again brings it back with its word and clip. After five rounds, a summary slide: the eight words, the goal with a tick, and "Classroom → Mark as done".
- **Say:**
  - "Close your eyes! … Open! What's missing?" The class shouts the word. "Yes! River!" All do the gesture. Five rounds.
  - "How many new words today? Eight! Say all eight with your hands, fast!"
  - "Read it: 'I can say, read and spell eight nature words.' Thumbs up, middle or down: show me!"
  - "How many stars in your last check? Show me with your fingers."
  - "Open your screen. Press 'Mark as done'. Close your laptop."
- **Praise:** "Eight new words in one lesson. You worked hard on every trail. I am proud of you!"

### The placement quiz

Questions 1-6 are built (index.html:201-206); 7 and 8 are new. The first answer counts, a miss just moves on, and nothing ever says "wrong" (index.html:389-391). Each of the eight words is the answer once.

| # | The kid gets | Presses | Counts as |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | hears "sea" | 1 of 3 pictures | warm-up, not scored |
| 2 | hears "mountain" | 1 of 3 pictures | warm-up, not scored |
| 3 | forest picture | 1 of 4 words | word question |
| 4 | grass picture | 1 of 4 words | word question |
| 5 | hears "river" | 1 of 6 words | word question |
| 6 | hears "dry" | 1 of 6 words | word question |
| 7 | "Birds fly in the ___." | air / sea / grass / river | sentence question |
| 8 | "I love ___: rivers, forests and mountains." | nature / dry / air / grass | sentence question |

- **Scoring:**
  - Mountain: both sentence questions right and at least 3 of the 4 word questions.
  - Forest: at least 3 of the 4 word questions.
  - River: everything else.
- **Why not the built rule:** it counts all six questions in thirds (index.html:208-209). After stage 2 nearly every kid gets the two listening questions, so a non-reader needs one lucky guess in four to reach Forest. That happens 61% of the time; under the rule above it is 3%.
- **When in doubt it places lower.** Moving up after a streak is a happy event; moving down is not. This is my pick.
- **Reveal, same size and confetti for all three:** "Your trail is River! Well done!" with the Hebrew trail name and Start ▶ (built, index.html:395-403).
- **Override:** no free pick at the start. The kid decides later whether to accept an earned offer, and the teacher can move anyone by typing `#level-1`, `-2` or `-3` at the end of the address (index.html:513).
- Research reason: when the program narrows the options and the learner chooses among them, learning holds and interest rises (Corbalan, Kester & van Merriënboer 2008). [inferred]
- Full learner control adds almost nothing to learning (Karich, Burns & Maki 2014 meta-analysis). [inferred]
- Learners who fear failing pick tasks that are too easy or far too hard (Atkinson 1957). [inferred]
- **Research is silent** on how many questions a two-minute placement into three groups needs. The cut scores are my pick.

### The earned offer to change trail

- **Up:** 5 questions in a row right on the first try. Card: "Wow! Five in a row! Try a new trail?" with buttons "Forest trail ▶" and "Stay here".
- **Down:** 3 questions in a row with a mistake. Card: "Want to try another trail?" plus "רוצים לנסות מסלול אחר?" and the same two buttons.
- **Wording:** keep the built lines, which say neither "harder" nor "easier" (index.html:218). His draft wording used both, and those are the labels differentiation.md §7 says to avoid.
- **Rules:**
  - the streak carries across games;
  - the quiz and the Last check never trigger an offer;
  - each direction is offered once per trail;
  - accepting lands on the same game number in the new trail, and stars stay.
- **Basis:** the counts are a pick. Research gives only the shape: practice at about 80-85% success.
- That figure is from Rosenshine 2012 and Wilson et al. 2019. [inferred]
- "N right in a row" is a common mastery rule in tutoring software (ASSISTments uses 3). [inferred]
- The built values are 6 up and 3 down (index.html:212). With the streak reset at every game (index.html:411), 6 of 8 allows a slip only in the first two questions.
