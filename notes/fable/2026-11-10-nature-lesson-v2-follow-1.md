  - "Today: eight NEW nature words. Read with me." The class reads the goal.

**Stage 2: Meet the words (standing).**
- Projector: word cards in the built order: nature, mountain, river, forest, sea, grass, air, dry (index.html:225-233). Then all eight pictures without words.
- Routine per word, about 35 seconds:
  1. "Look. Listen." Press Space: the clip plays and the word appears.
  2. Say it twice with the gesture.
  3. "Everybody!" three times: normal, whisper, loud.
  4. "What is it in Hebrew?" The class answers.
- Gestures:
  - nature: open your arms wide
  - mountain: hands meet in a peak
  - river: hand swims like a snake
  - forest: arms up like trees
  - sea: big waves with both arms
  - grass: wiggle your fingers low
  - air: blow on your hand
  - dry: wipe your forehead
- Two games on the pictures slide:
  - **Show me!** "I say the word. You show me with your hands. No talking!"
  - **Say it!** "I show, you say." End with a trick: point at the mountain and ask "Sea?" The class answers "No! Mountain!"
- Every kid: says each word at least four times with the gesture.
- Transition: "Sit down. Don't open the laptop yet. Look at the screen."
- Praise: "Beautiful gestures, and I heard everyone's voice!"

- Projector: the three places; then demo one quiz question and one "Listen. Say the word." on the kid page.

- Praise: "Quick and quiet. Thank you!"

**Stage 4: Play and race.**

### The quiz and placement

Built as specified (index.html:296-311): eight questions, each word the answer once, first answer counts, nothing ever says "wrong".

| # | The kid gets | Presses | Counts as |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1-2 | hears "sea", "mountain" | 1 of 3 pictures | warm-up |
| 3-4 | forest, grass picture | 1 of 4 words | word |
| 5-6 | hears "river", "dry" | 1 of 6 words | word |
| 7 | "Birds fly in the ___." | air / sea / grass / river | sentence |
| 8 | "I love ___: rivers, forests and mountains." | nature / dry / air / grass | sentence |

- **Scoring:** Mountain needs both sentences and 3 of 4 words; Forest needs 3 of 4 words; otherwise River. A guessing non-reader reaches Forest about 3% of the time.
- With near-beginners, expect few or no kids on Mountain. That is fine.
- **Override:** no free pick. The kid accepts or declines each offer, and the teacher can move him with `#level-1`, `-2` or `-3` in the address.
- When the program narrows the options and the learner chooses among them, learning holds and interest rises (Corbalan, Kester & van Merriënboer 2008). [inferred]
- Full learner control adds almost nothing to learning (Karich, Burns & Maki 2014). [inferred]
- Low performers overestimate what they can do (Kruger & Dunning 1999). [inferred]

### The games (question a)

Eight rounds per game, one per word.

| Game | Phase | River | Forest | Mountain |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1. Listen. Say the word. | repetition | picture, word, Hebrew word; record 3 s; "Hear me" | same | picture and sentence; record 6 s |
| 2. Listen. Press… | recognition | 3 pictures | 6 pictures | 6 written words |
| 3. Look / Read. Press the word. | recognition | picture → 3 words, speaker on each | picture → 4 words | sentence gap → 4 words |
| 4. Build the word. | reproduction | first letter given, short words first | all letters | 2 extra letters, no speaker |
| 🏁 Last check | check | 4 questions | 4 questions | 4 questions |

- All of this is built. One change: show the Hebrew word on the say card in all three places, not only River (index.html:268). The pictures for air, dry and nature cannot carry the meaning alone.
- **Weak kid succeeds:** River always has sound, three options and a first letter.
- **Strong kid is stretched:** Mountain reads sentences and spells without sound among extra letters.
- **Backed:**
  - Practice by answering from memory helps in real classrooms (Yang, Luo, Vadillo, Yu & Shanks 2021, meta-analysis; Agarwal, Nunes & Blunt 2021, review). [inferred]
  - Learners can recognise a word before they can recall it (González-Fernández & Schmitt 2020; Laufer & Goldstein 2004). [inferred]
  - Saying words aloud helps children remember them (Icht & Mama 2015). [inferred]
  - Digital games help second-language vocabulary (Thompson & von Gillern 2020, meta-analysis). [inferred]
- **Picks:** these exact games, eight rounds, record-and-playback.

### Offers to move (question b)

- **Up:** 5 first tries in a row. **Down:** 3 questions in a row with a miss.
- Each is offered once per place. The streak carries across games. A move keeps the game number, points and cards. All of this is built (index.html:630-656).
- **Why these counts (my arithmetic, not research):**
  - Five lucky guesses in a row on three-option questions happen 0.4% of the time.
  - A well-placed kid (80% first try) misses three in a row under 1% of the time; a kid at 40% does so about 22% of the time. So the down signal is sharp.
  - The up signal is not sharp: most well-placed kids also hit five in a row at some point. That is why it is a question, asked once, and never automatic.
- Practice works best at roughly 85% success (Wilson et al. 2019); that study used simple learning models, not children. [inferred]

### Rewards (question d)

**What helps, and what is noise.**
- Gamification has a small-to-medium positive effect on learning (Sailer & Homner 2020, meta-analysis; Bai, Hew & Huang 2020, meta-analysis). [inferred]
- In English-as-a-foreign-language studies the usual set is points, levels, badges and feedback, mostly with gains in vocabulary and motivation (Zhang & Hasim 2023, review). [inferred]
- Decoration unrelated to the task lowers learning (Sundararajan & Adesope 2020, meta-analysis). [inferred]
- Lively feedback raises appeal but not performance, and too much lowers it (Hicks et al. 2019; Kao 2020). [inferred]
- Loot boxes are linked to problem gambling in adolescents (Zendle, Meyer & Over 2019). [inferred]
- Rewards that inform ("you got it") do not harm motivation; rewards handed out as payment do (Deci, Koestner & Ryan 1999). [inferred]
- Research is silent on combo sizes and level spacing. The numbers below are picks.

**The set to build tonight.**

| Element | Rule | Built today |
|---|---|---|
| Points | say a word +5; right first try +10; right after a miss +5 | nothing after a miss (index.html:640) |
| Combo | ×2 from 3 in a row, ×3 from 5 in a row; a miss resets it and takes nothing | grows to ×5 (index.html:318) |
| Levels | Level 2 at 50 points, then one per 100 | one per 150 (index.html:322) |
| Cards | one per word, three stars: said, found, built; three stars turn it gold | a card only after three clean first tries (index.html:555) |
| Comeback | a missed word returns once at the end of its game; finishing it gives the star | not built |
| Race points | +5 per right answer, no combo; +50 for a new best | full combo points (index.html:714) |

- **Why change the built numbers:**
  - With ×5, a strong kid earns up to 300 a game and a kid who needs two tries earns 0.
  - Levels then arrive every three answers for one kid and never for another.
  - With the new numbers the range is about 60 to 180 a game. A weak kid still levels up two or three times; a strong kid about six.
- **Level-up moments:** a 1.6-second "Level 3!" card, a short fanfare and confetti (built, index.html:563). At ×3 the flame shows "ON FIRE" and the up offer appears.
- **Sounds, all synthesized, no files:**
  - right answer: two rising notes, stepping higher through a combo;
  - miss: one soft low note;
  - star: a ding;
  - gold card: a three-note sparkle;
  - level-up: a fanfare;
  - race: three countdown beeps, a tick in the last five seconds, a horn at the end.
- **Motion:** only after an answer, under one second. Confetti only on reveal, level-up, gold card, finish and new best.
- **Avoid, exactly:**
  - random prizes, mystery boxes, spins, or rewards on an unpredictable schedule;
  - "so close!" teasers;
  - losing points, lives or "game over";
  - timers outside the race;
  - background music;
  - motion while a question is on screen;
  - a shop or spendable coins;
  - a full ranking.
- **Cool, not babyish (pick; research is silent):** keep the built bar with level, points and flame (index.html:534-538); bold flat icons, no mascot, no baby praise. The fonts are in: Fredoka and Rubik, inlined (index.html:10-11).

### The race (question c)

**Research.**
- Competition inside learning games has a positive effect overall (Chen, Shih & Law 2020, meta-analysis). [inferred]
- It works best combined with a shared goal (Sailer & Homner 2020). [inferred]
- Its net effect on performance is near zero, because it lifts kids who aim to win and hurts kids who fear losing (Murayama & Elliot 2012, meta-analysis). [inferred]
- Low positions on a board hurt motivation (Bai, Hew, Sailer & Jia 2021). [inferred]
- A visible top score works like a hard goal (Landers, Bauer & Callan 2017). [inferred]

**Design.**
- **When:** after the Last check, on the finish screen (built). Add a small locked chip in the top bar from the start, "🏆 🔒 after 🏁", so the boy who loves winning sees the goal early. Racing first would mean guessing at words he has not learned.
- **Flag at 11 minutes, not 12** (index.html:293). With an 18-minute stage every kid gets about five minutes of race time, not only the fast ones.
- **30 seconds, not 60 (pick):** in five minutes that is about eight tries instead of four, so more kids see "New best!". One setting; built at 20 (index.html:327).
- **One race for all places:** picture → 1 of 4 written words, the eight words in shuffled sets. Today each place races on its own questions (index.html:696), so River would win a shared board on easier questions.
- **One press per question:** a wrong press shows the answer and freezes for one second. Today a kid can press every option until one is right (index.html:703).
- **The card:** trophy and stopwatch, "RACE! 30 s", "Can you win?", the top score, "Your best", GO. "Try again" is always there after a race.
- **Healthy by design:**
  - "Your best" is on the card and "New best!" is celebrated for everyone.
  - The board is top 5 only, one row per kid, with no place shown.
  - The projector shows the board only in the closing.
  - The teacher praises new bests, not rank.

  - If a request takes over two seconds, the card silently shows the fallback.
- **Fallback, also the default tonight:** the card shows "Teacher: 12". Many kids can beat a fixed score, so nobody has to lose. Aviv plays one race tonight to set the number.
- **Names (change to item 3):** a top-5 score asks the kid to pick a game name from two lists, plus an automatic number: "Wild Sea 27".
