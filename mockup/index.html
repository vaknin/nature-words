Questions, each with my pick (answer only where you disagree)

  1. Look: A, B or C? My pick: A: most fun for 5th graders and the scene itself teaches the words; B's three-card clarity is built into it (one label, one Start button per pin).
  2. Do kids see their level? My pick: no. Same title "Nature Quest" for all; the level is only in the link and in your Classroom view. Animal names (turtle/fox/eagle) tend to read as a ranking.
  3. Who goes to which level? My pick: you sort them from what you and the class teacher know (about a third each); no placement test, it eats lesson time.
  4. Choice inside a level: the research says matching kids to "learning styles" (art kid, logic kid, talk kid) doesn't improve learning; what works is levels by readiness plus a small free choice. My pick: every kid picks 1 of 3 (draw / solve / say) inside his level.
  5. Hebrew help: My pick: level 1 always sees a short Hebrew line, level 2 one tap away, level 3 none.
  6. Headphones: do the kids have them? My pick: assume yes (30 laptops talking at once is chaos); if not, sound stays on the projector only and the laptops are silent text + pictures.
  7. "Say it" recording: the laptop records the kid and plays it back to him, nothing saved or sent. My pick: yes (needs one "Allow microphone" click). Or skip recording and just talk to a partner.
  8. Exit ticket: My pick: a 3-question check at the end of the site + "Mark as done" in Classroom (you see who finished live). Alternative: a Google Forms quiz assignment.
  9. Student Groups: does your school's Classroom show a "Groups" tab when you assign? If not, you tick names for each level (2 minutes, done once). My pick: tick names; works on every licence.
  10. Voices: My pick: English clips only, in letter-b's boy voice you liked plus a matching girl, each checked by speech recognition; no 5-takes picker this time (shorter lesson, and the Hebrew voices failed last time; Hebrew stays as text).
